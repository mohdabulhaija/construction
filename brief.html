Paediatric health guidance for parents.

A parent describes what they are seeing in their child. Within minutes they get a written report that explains what it means, what to do tonight, and the exact point at which to seek care. One annual fee covers every child in the household.

01

Product overview

Bedside is a subscription service for parents of children aged 0 to 15.

The parent fills in a short form: which child, what they have noticed, in their own words, and how long it has been going on. A few minutes later a report comes back with the same four parts every time.

Part 1What you told usTheir own words, read back, so a misunderstanding is visible immediately.
Part 2What it likely meansPlain language, written for that child's age.
Part 3What to do nowSpecific actions for tonight, each with the reason.
Part 4When to worryNumbered thresholds, not vague symptoms.

Every report ends with one next step, not an upsell: book fifteen minutes with a paediatrician. One call a year is included in the subscription.

02

The problem we solve

It is eleven at night. The child has a fever. The parent has three options and none of them work.

  • Search the internet. Worst-case results, written for nobody in particular. It raises the anxiety without settling anything.
  • Wait for a doctor. Hours on a helpline or days for an appointment. The moment the parent needed help has already passed.
  • Go to the emergency room. Hours of waiting and AED 800 to 2,000, often for something that turns out not to be an emergency.

The gap is not a lack of information. There is more paediatric information available than ever. The gap is that nothing turns what the parent is looking at into a decision they can act on tonight.

That is the whole product. Not more information. A decision, and the line at which the decision changes.

03

Target audience

Primary Parents of children 0 to 4 Highest illness frequency and lowest parental confidence. First-time parents most of all. This is where the anxiety lives and where the habit forms.
Expansion Parents of children 5 to 15 Lower frequency, but the subscription is already paid and the household already has the habit. Retention play, not acquisition.

Who actually buys. Usually the most anxious parent in the household. One decision-maker, one payment, and the whole family is covered, which removes any argument about whether it is worth adding another child.

Who else uses it. The other parent, grandparents, and anyone else looking after the children. Access is by household, not by individual.

The main value

A clear answer in minutes, at the moment the parent needs it.

Value 1It explainsWhat is most likely happening, in plain language, for that child's age.
Value 2It instructsWhat to do tonight and tomorrow. Concrete, with the reason attached.
Value 3It draws the lineThe exact threshold at which to seek care. Numbers, not feelings.
The third one is the real product. Every parent already knows to watch their child. Nobody tells them what they are watching for. Turning an open-ended worry into a line you can check against is what converts anxiety into sleep.
05

Competitive edge

The alternativeWhat it leaves the parent withWhat Bedside does instead
Search and forums Worst-case answers with no way to judge relevance to their own child. One report, written for that child's age and what the parent actually described.
Symptom checkers A list of possible conditions, ending in a generic "see a doctor". A decision and a threshold, not a probability list.
Per-visit telehealth A fee per consultation, so parents ration usage and wait until they are worried enough. Flat annual fee, so they ask at the first worry instead of the fifth.
Public health lines Long waits, and triage built to protect the system rather than to inform the parent. Minutes, in writing, and the parent keeps the report.
What is hard to copy: the threshold library, age-banded and written to be checkable, plus a growing record of each child over time. Anyone can build a symptom form. The defensible asset is the accumulated judgement about where the lines sit, and the trust that comes from a parent having used it before and found it right.

Why flat, not per use. Usage-based pricing suppresses exactly the behaviour we want. A parent who is charged per report waits until they are frightened. A parent on a flat fee asks early, which is when the guidance is cheapest to give and most useful to receive. It also makes the product feel like insurance against a bad night rather than a purchase made in one.

Why per household, not per child. It removes the worst conversation in the funnel, which is a parent deciding which of their children to cover. It also makes a second child a retention event instead of an upsell.

Why AED 399. It is roughly one private paediatric consultation in Dubai, which starts at AED 300, and it breaks even against four insurance co-pays. It sits at half of Blueberry Pediatrics, the closest comparable anywhere, and clear of the regional teleconsult apps. Metering the paediatrician call is what keeps it viable: unlimited calls at UAE clinician rates would cost more than the subscription.

The number that matters is annual renewal. Everything else follows from whether the reports were right when it counted.

07

Main messaging

Anchor line Tell us what's happening. We'll tell you what it means.

Everything else supports this. It states the exchange in the parent's own terms and promises exactly one thing.

Pillar 1In your wordsThe parent does not need the right terms. The report reads their description back to them.
Pillar 2In plain languageNo jargon unless it is explained in the same sentence.
Pillar 3With a clear lineThresholds you can check, so you know when to act and when not to.

How we sound. Calm, plain, declarative. Short sentences. Never urgent or alarmed, because an anxious parent does not need us to raise the temperature. Not commercial in tone, because trust is the product.

CalmPlainDeclarativeShort sentences No exclamation marksNever alarmedNever salesy

What we never say. That we diagnose, that we replace a doctor, or anything that implies a parent should wait on us before calling for help. The footer says so plainly and it stays on every page.
