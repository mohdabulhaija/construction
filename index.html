A few minutes later
Your report Tuesday 8 September, 21:38

Amara, 2 years 4 months

A fever and a night-time cough, since yesterday

Highest temperature 38.6 °C Second day Written against guidance reviewed by paediatricians
Section 1

What you told us

You told us Amara has had a temperature since yesterday afternoon. Tonight it was 38.6. She is off her food, but she is still drinking her milk. She has a cough that is worse at night. She is clingy and tired, and she was still playing a bit this afternoon. She has had four or more wet nappies today. You gave her paracetamol at seven this evening. You cannot see a rash. Nobody else at home is ill.

If any of that is wrong, go back and correct it. The rest of this report is built on it.

Section 2

What it likely means

This looks like a common viral infection. A virus is a germ that antibiotics do not treat. Her body clears it on its own, usually within a week.

The fever is her body's response to the virus. It is not the illness itself. A fever at 38.6 is ordinary for a child her age with a virus. The number matters less than how she is in herself.

The cough is worse at night because mucus runs down the back of the throat when she lies flat. That is expected and it is not a sign the cough is getting worse.

Two things you told us matter most. She is still drinking. She was still playing. Those are the signs that tell you her body is coping.

Children under five typically have several infections like this a year. Going off food for a few days does no harm as long as she keeps drinking.

Section 3

What to do now

  • Offer milk or water every hour she is awake.Small amounts, often. A few sips count. Do not worry about food for now.
  • Give paracetamol only if she is uncomfortable.Use the dose on the packet for her weight, not her age. Leave at least four hours between doses, and no more than four doses in twenty-four hours. Treat how she feels, not the number on the thermometer.
  • Dress her in one light layer.Keep the room cool. Do not point a fan at her and do not sponge her with cold water. Both make her shiver, which raises her temperature.
  • Check on her once during the night.You are looking at her breathing and her colour, not her temperature. Set an alarm if you need to.
  • Count wet nappies tomorrow.Four or more in the day is fine. Fewer than three is a threshold in the next section.
  • Keep her home tomorrow.She can go back to nursery twenty-four hours after her temperature settles without paracetamol.
Section 4

When to worry

Each of these is a line, not a feeling. If she crosses one, act on it. If she crosses none, what you are doing is enough.

Fewer than 3
wet nappies in twenty-four hours, or no wet nappy for twelve hours.
5 days
of temperature at 38 or above, without a full day below it.
6 hours
without keeping any fluid down.
Breathing
that pulls the skin in between or under her ribs, or is fast enough that she cannot feed or talk.
A rash
that does not fade when you press the side of a clear glass firmly against it.
Hard to wake
or awake but not responding to you the way she usually does.
Cold hands and feet
while her body is hot, with pale, grey, or blotchy skin.

Fewer nappies, five days of fever, or six hours without fluid: arrange for her to be seen the same day.

Breathing, a rash that does not fade, hard to wake, or cold hands and feet: call an ambulance. Do not wait to see if it passes.
