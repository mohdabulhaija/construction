The problem

It is eleven at night. The child has a fever.

The parent has three options. None of them work.

Option 1Search the internet Free Worst-case results, written for nobody in particular. It raises the anxiety without settling anything.
Option 2Wait for a doctor AED 300, days later Hours on a helpline, or days for an appointment. The moment the parent needed help has already passed.
Option 3Go to the emergency room AED 800–2,000 Hours of waiting, often for something that turns out not to be an emergency.

The gap is not a lack of information. Nothing turns what the parent is looking at into a decision they can act on tonight.

What it is

A parent describes what they see. A report comes back.

A short form: which child, what you have noticed, in your own words, how long it has been going on. Minutes later, the same four parts, every time.

Part 1What you told usTheir own words, read back, so a misunderstanding is visible at once.
Part 2What it likely meansPlain language, written for that child's age.
Part 3What to do nowSpecific actions for tonight, each with the reason.
Part 4When to worryNumbered thresholds, not vague symptoms.

Every report ends with one next step, not an upsell: book fifteen minutes with a paediatrician.

Who it is for

Parents of children, newborn to fifteen.

What is ordinary at six months is not ordinary at ten years. Every report is written for the age of the child it is about.

Under 1BabiesFeeding, weight, crying, breathing, temperature. Tighter thresholds, and we say so.
1 to 4ToddlersFevers, coughs, rashes, tummy upsets. The core market: highest frequency, lowest confidence.
5 to 11School ageEars, throats, headaches, sleep, days off school, falls.
12 to 15TeenagersGrowth, mood, sleep, headaches, sports injuries.
Who buysUsually the most anxious parent in the household. One decision-maker, one payment, the whole family covered.
Who usesThe other parent, grandparents, and anyone else looking after the children. Access is by household.

Why flat, not per use. A parent charged per report waits until they are frightened. A parent on a flat fee asks early, which is when guidance is cheapest to give and most useful to receive.

11
Before this is a plan

What we still have to prove.

Everything above is reasoned from market data. None of it is yet evidence from our own customers.

  • Price. AED 399 is benchmarked, not tested. Run AED 299, 399 and 499 as a live landing-page test before locking it.
  • Cost per report. Depends on how much clinician review each one needs. It decides whether unlimited is viable at this price.
  • Call attach rate. One call is included, so it is the largest cost line in the model.
  • Regulatory position. DHA and DoH licensing for the call, and where guidance ends and a regulated medical device begins.
  • Renewal. The whole model rests on it, and we have no evidence yet. It is what the founding cohort is for.
