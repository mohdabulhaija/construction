One rule that shapes all of it

We do not use fear to sell calm.

The standard playbook for this category is alarm: what most parents get wrong, the sign you are missing, the mistake that could cost you. It works on the feed and it is wrong for us. Our entire promise is that we will not raise a parent's temperature. A brand that markets by frightening people cannot then be trusted to settle them.

So the hook is recognition, not alarm. A parent stops scrolling because they see their own eleven-at-night described exactly, not because we have told them their child might be dying. Recognition holds attention just as well and it is the only hook that survives contact with the product.

Before any of this goes live

Two things to clear first. Neither is optional.
  1. A paediatrician signs off every clinical line. The thresholds above are written from standard paediatric guidance and they are the right shape, but posting clinical content to thousands of parents is a different risk from putting it in a report a parent asked for. Get them reviewed and keep a dated record of who approved what.
  2. Health marketing is regulated in the UAE. DHA and DoH have rules on how health services advertise, and a licensed provider's promotional content can require approval. Check where guidance content sits before the first post, not after it.

Keep the safety line on every clinical post: general guidance, not advice about your child, and call an ambulance if you think it is serious. It is not legal cover, it is the voice being consistent.

04

If someone else writes the next batch

Four tests. A post fails if any one of them fails.

Test 1Would it frighten a parent who is already frightened?If yes, rewrite it. This is the one that kills most drafts.
Test 2Is there a number or a specific in it?"Watch for dehydration" is nothing. "Fewer than three wet nappies" is the product.
Test 3Does it scold?No "most parents get this wrong". Parents are doing their best at three in the morning.
Test 4Read it aloud. Does it sound like a person?No exclamation marks. No "here's the thing". No asking for comments.
